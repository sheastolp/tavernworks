The AI Pool

One AI model, split across a pool of ordinary PCs — bigger than any one of them could run alone.

Most home PCs top out around a 4-billion-parameter AI model. The pool links machines together so a single much larger model — today a 14B — can be spread across all of them, each PC carrying part of it. Next up: Qwen3.5's 35B mixture-of-experts model, which only works ~3B of its weights per word and so runs far faster than its size suggests. It's the mining-pool idea, pointed at AI instead of coins.

How it works

  1. One model, many PCs. The model is sliced by layers. Each enrolled PC holds some slices in memory; a request flows through them in order.
  2. The operator holds the ends. The first slice (which sees your words) and the last slice (which produces the reply) always stay on the operator's own machines. Contributor PCs only ever hold middle layers.
  3. Workers dial out. The worker app connects to the coordinator — no open ports on your PC — and the operator approves every machine before it can hold any part of the model.
  4. Your PC stays yours. The worker yields the moment you need your machine: gaming, fullscreen apps, low memory, on battery, or a manual pause. Leaving is a graceful drain, never a yank.

Join the pool

Joining is open, and it's three steps:

  1. Get an account. Register with the button below. The operator approves accounts by hand; approval comes with 100 starter shares.
  2. Run the worker on the PC or phone you're contributing (Windows, Linux or Android, with Python 3 — that's the only requirement):
    Windows, in Command Prompt: curl -fsSL https://pool.tavernworks.dev/downloads/install-worker.bat -o install-worker.bat && install-worker.bat
    Linux, in a terminal: curl -fsSL https://pool.tavernworks.dev/downloads/install-worker.sh -o install-worker.sh && bash install-worker.sh
    Android, in Termux (phone plugged in): curl -fsSL https://pool.tavernworks.dev/downloads/install-android.sh -o install-android.sh && bash install-android.sh
    Phones with a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 or newer (Adreno 7xx/8xx) lend their GPU when the pool's model allows it; every other phone lends RAM and CPU.
    It downloads the worker from the pool's own download shelf, enrolls your machine with a sensible memory offer (override it with --offered-mb if you like), and waits. The operator approves every machine by hand too — just leave it running; it carries on by itself. Your PC dials out to the pool; nothing about it is exposed to the internet.
  3. Link the machine to your account. Once approved, the worker prints a personal link. Open it, save your account key on that page, and click Link machine — the form fills itself in. From then on your PC earns shares for your account whenever it's in the pool.

Contributors earn shares for the memory and hours their PC puts in, and spend shares to use the pool themselves — through the web chat, a personal API key for the pool's OpenAI-shaped endpoint, or MCP (point a client at https://pool.tavernworks.dev/mcp with the same key).

Mining pools publish their stats; so does this one. Contributing earns shares, using the pool spends them, and every movement is written to an append-only ledger (who, when, how many tokens — never what was said).

Privacy & safety, in plain words

Middle layers see numbers, not readable text — but they aren't magic either, so the design keeps the sensitive ends on the operator's hardware and says so openly. Worker software is open source, holds its slices in RAM only, and every download is verified against a published SHA-256 checksum before it runs. The pool's front door keeps an accounting ledger of who used how many tokens — prompts and replies themselves are not logged.
